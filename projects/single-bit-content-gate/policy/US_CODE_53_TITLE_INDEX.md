# U.S. Code: 53-title scope index for congressional outreach

**Dated 2026-11-12. Public evaluation draft, not legal advice.** The official [Office of the Law Revision Counsel catalog](https://uscode.house.gov/download.html) lists 54 numbered U.S. Code titles through Public Law 119-138 (November 3, 2026), with **Title 53 reserved**. Thus **53 active titles** are shown below. This is a *subject-matter index*, not 53 cited statutory sections, jurisdictional findings, or grounds for automatic support by any lawmaker.

For an actual legislative referral or sponsor letter, cite only the **Core** or specifically supported **Context** authorities; do not append irrelevant titles as if they conferred authority. This index was drawn from the official title headings; no section-by-section validity check was performed for the non-core titles. Congress may refer a bill to committees by its actual content rather than by the number of citations. The operative bill keeps narrow statutory anchors.

## Actual statutory anchors for the proposal

1. [18 U.S.C. § 2258A](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t18/s2258A/) covers provider reporting when statutory actual-knowledge conditions are met; subsection (f) does **not** impose general monitoring or proactive scanning. Avoid a rule that weakens those duties.
2. [18 U.S.C. § 2702](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t18/s2702/) limits voluntary disclosure of stored communications subject to statutory exceptions; raw archive uploads are not assumed lawful.
3. [47 U.S.C. § 230](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t47/s230/) addresses private blocking and screening and preserves other areas of law; it does not certify this filter.
4. [15 U.S.C. § 6501](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t15/s6501/) defines a child as under 13 **for COPPA**, which is not equivalent to this project's age-related keyword category.
5. [5 U.S.C. §§ 552–553](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t5/s552/) concern agency public information and applicable rulemaking; neither currently mandates publication of this proposed gate.
6. [44 U.S.C. § 3506](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t44/s3506/) addresses federal information management and dissemination.
7. [2 U.S.C. § 285b](https://uscode.house.gov/us/usc/t2/s285b/) concerns the OLRC's U.S. Code functions; it does not give sponsors extra authority merely because more titles are listed.

## Legislative drafting guardrails

- These 53 headings are for **outreach mapping**, not **53 amendments** and not 53 legally applicable mandates.
- Congress should be asked to consider one voluntary public-logic transparency pilot, not to adopt a predetermined regex as law.
- Independently reported danger, human review, statutory reporting, data protection, accessibility, and due process are **outside** the HOLD bit and must never be eliminated by Boolean algebra.
- No universal Internet driver's license, use of JWICS, monitoring mandate, or authorization to share suspected CSAM with analytics vendors is proposed.
- Public versions, differences, dissent, and agency attestations remain public if the proposal is enacted, but private records and exploit-sensitive specifics do not.
